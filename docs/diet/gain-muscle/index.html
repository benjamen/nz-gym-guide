The Truth About Building Muscle (No BS)

You cannot build muscle without:

  • A calorie surplus — you need to eat more than you burn. A modest 250–500 cal surplus minimises fat gain.
  • Enough protein — 1.6–2.2g per kg bodyweight daily. For an 80 kg person, that's 128–176g protein per day.
  • Progressive overload in the gym — you must lift heavier or do more reps over time. No amount of food builds muscle without this stimulus.

That's it. Supplements help at the margins. The three points above are 95% of the result.

Timing Your Nutrition Around Training

Timing matters less than total daily intake — but these habits help optimise muscle growth:

  • 🕐 Pre-workout (1–2 hrs before): Carbs + moderate protein. Example: banana + Greek yoghurt, or rice + chicken.
  • ⚡ Post-workout (within 2 hrs): 30–40g protein + carbs. Example: protein shake + banana, or chicken + rice.
  • 💤 Before bed: Casein protein (cottage cheese, Greek yoghurt) — slow-release protein feeds muscles overnight.
  • ⏰ Spread protein across meals: 4–5 meals with 30–40g protein each maximises muscle protein synthesis.
  • ☕ Pre-workout coffee: 200mg caffeine 30–60 min before training improves strength by 3–5% on average.

Supplements Worth Taking for Muscle Gain

Most supplements are unnecessary if your diet is right. These three have the best evidence:

  • 💪 Creatine monohydrate — The most studied supplement in sports science. Increases strength 5–15%, accelerates muscle growth. Take 3–5g daily. Cost: ~$20–40/month at Chemist Warehouse NZ.
  • 🥛 Whey protein powder — Not magic, just convenient protein. Use to hit your daily protein target when food alone isn't enough. Cost: ~$30–60/kg. Pak'nSave often has the best prices.
  • ☕ Caffeine (coffee or supplement) — Proven performance enhancer. 200mg pre-workout improves reps, reduces fatigue. Cheapest as instant coffee — $5–10/month.

Common Muscle-Gain Mistakes in NZ

  • Not eating enough — most beginners eat at maintenance and wonder why they're not growing. Track for 2 weeks to check.
  • Too much cardio — marathon training burns the calories you need for muscle. Keep cardio to 2–3 moderate sessions/week max.
  • Changing programmes every 4 weeks — trust a simple programme for 12–16 weeks. Results require consistency.
  • Skipping legs — lower body muscles are the biggest in the body. Squats and deadlifts raise testosterone and grow muscle everywhere.
  • Underestimating sleep — 90% of muscle repair happens during sleep. 7–9 hours is not optional for muscle gain.
  • Only using machines — free weights (barbell, dumbbells) activate more muscle fibres and build more functional strength.
