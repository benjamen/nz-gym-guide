Les Mills vs City Fitness — Which NZ Gym Is Worth Your Money?

Les Mills and City Fitness are New Zealand's most contrasting gym chains. Les Mills is premium, class-focused, and locked-in. City Fitness is budget, flexible, and available 24/7. If you want group fitness in a premium environment, Les Mills wins. If you want maximum flexibility, 24/7 access, and lowest cost, City Fitness is unbeatable.

Price Comparison: Budget vs Premium

City Fitness starts at $7.99/week, making it NZ's cheapest mainstream gym. Les Mills starts at $17.99/week — over double the price. Over 3 months, City Fitness costs roughly $107 (with $29 joining fee = $136 total), while Les Mills costs around $237 plus joining fee = $286. City Fitness is significantly cheaper, but you're paying for flexibility and 24/7 access, not premium facilities or premium classes. Les Mills' higher price reflects pool, sauna, and professional group fitness instruction.

Contracts and Flexibility

City Fitness offers multiple options: 4-week minimum commitment or true no-lock-in ($9.99/week). Les Mills requires 3 months minimum. If you're testing a gym, City Fitness is lower-risk — you can commit for just 4 weeks. If you hate Les Mills after 3 months, you're locked in. City Fitness wins on flexibility; Les Mills wins if you know you'll commit longer.

24/7 Access vs Staffed Hours

City Fitness is 24/7. You can train at midnight, 5am, Christmas Day, or during a power cut. Les Mills operates around 5:30am–10pm weekdays with reduced weekend hours. If you're a shift worker, night owl, or early bird, City Fitness is the only viable option. Les Mills members must fit training into staffed hours — no exceptions.

Group Fitness Classes

Les Mills' entire offering is built around group fitness — BodyPump, RPM, BODYATTACK, BODYCOMBAT, BodyBalance, GRIT, and more. Classes are high-production, music-licensed, and instructor-certified to a global standard. City Fitness offers group fitness classes but with less production value and fewer timetable slots. If group fitness is your primary motivation, Les Mills is incomparably better. If you prefer solo training with occasional group options, City Fitness is sufficient.

Facilities: Pools, Saunas, Crèches

Les Mills includes pools and saunas at most locations, plus crèches at select clubs — ideal for families and swimmers. City Fitness doesn't offer pools, saunas, or crèches. This is the main amenity gap. If you swim or need childcare while you train, Les Mills is necessary. If you just need equipment and classes, City Fitness is adequate.

Best For Shift Workers and Night Owls

City Fitness owns this category completely. Shift workers, night-shift nurses, overnight security, and anyone who trains outside 6am–9pm will find Les Mills unsuitable. City Fitness is the only mainstream chain offering 24/7 access with affordability.

Who Should Choose City Fitness?

  • Anyone on a tight budget (under $10/week)
  • Shift workers and night owls (24/7 access non-negotiable)
  • People who want complete flexibility to cancel after 4 weeks
  • Members who don't need pools, saunas, or professional group fitness
  • Those wanting multiple club access on a low budget

Who Should Choose Les Mills?

  • Group fitness lovers who rely on classes for motivation
  • Swimmers who need pool access
  • Families needing crèche support while training
  • Anyone under 23 (20% discount makes it more affordable)
  • People willing to pay for premium facilities and professional instruction

The Verdict

City Fitness is better for budget-conscious members, shift workers, and anyone wanting no-lock-in flexibility. Les Mills is better for group fitness lovers, swimmers, and families who can commit to premium pricing. These aren't competitors in the same market — City Fitness is the no-frills budget workhorse; Les Mills is the premium class-focused choice. Your decision should be based on priorities: is 24/7 access and price your priority, or premium classes and facilities?
