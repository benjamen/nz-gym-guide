How to Cancel Anytime Fitness NZ — Step-by-Step Guide 2026

Cancelling an Anytime Fitness membership in New Zealand is straightforward but requires you to follow the right process — giving written notice, meeting your contract terms, and cancelling direct debits at the right time. This guide walks you through exactly what to do.

Step 1 — Check Your Contract End Date

Log into the Anytime Fitness app or find your original membership agreement to confirm when your 12-month minimum term ends. Cancelling before this date usually requires paying out the remainder of your contract. Anytime Fitness NZ clubs are franchises — exact early cancellation fees can vary between clubs. Call your home club to confirm the exact terms.

Step 2 — Give Written Notice

Anytime Fitness requires written notice to cancel. The most reliable method is submitting a cancellation form at your home club in person. You can also send a written request via email to your club (find the club contact details on the Anytime Fitness website). Notice periods are typically 30 days — meaning if you give notice today, you'll continue to be charged for approximately one more month. Keep a copy of any written notice you submit.

Step 3 — Get Written Confirmation

Ask the club to confirm your cancellation in writing — either by email or a signed cancellation form. Do not assume the cancellation has been processed without this confirmation. If you can't get written confirmation immediately, follow up within 3 business days.

Step 4 — Cancel Your Direct Debit (After Confirmation)

Only cancel your direct debit after you have written confirmation that your membership is cancelled AND you have paid all outstanding amounts. Cancelling your direct debit first while fees are still owed can result in a debt being sent to a collection agency. Once you have confirmation and all payments are settled, you can cancel the direct debit via your bank's online banking.

Cancelling During the Contract Period

If you want to leave before your 12-month contract ends, your options are: 1. **Pay the early exit fee** — typically the remaining contract payments. This can be several hundred dollars if you're cancelling early in the contract. 2. **Medical or injury cancellation** — if a health issue prevents you from exercising, most Anytime Fitness clubs will waive or reduce early exit fees with appropriate documentation (doctor's letter). 3. **Relocation** — if you're moving to an area with no Anytime Fitness, some clubs will waive early exit fees. This is at club discretion — ask your home club. 4. **Transfer your membership** — some clubs allow you to transfer your membership to another person to avoid the early exit fee.

Common Cancellation Problems to Avoid

  • Cancelling your direct debit without formal membership cancellation — this leads to debt collection
  • Not getting written confirmation — always get a paper trail
  • Missing the notice period — if your contract ends June 30 and notice is 30 days, you need to give notice by June 1
  • Cancelling through the app only — some clubs don't process app cancellations; confirm in person or via email
  • Assuming moving suburbs is enough — you need to cancel formally regardless of where you live

Your Rights Under NZ Consumer Law

Anytime Fitness memberships are covered by the Consumer Guarantees Act 1993 and Fair Trading Act 1986. Key rights:

  • All fees and terms must have been clearly disclosed at signup. If they weren't, you may be able to cancel without penalty.
  • Early exit fees must be a genuine estimate of loss — not a punitive charge. Excessive fees may be challengeable at the Disputes Tribunal.
  • Automatic contract renewal clauses require clear notice. If your contract renewed without explicit notification, this may be a Fair Trading Act breach.

If you're facing a dispute, contact the NZ Commerce Commission at comcom.govt.nz or use the Disputes Tribunal for claims under $30,000.

If You Have a Problem

If your club isn't processing your cancellation correctly, escalate in writing. Contact the Anytime Fitness NZ head office if your club isn't responding. If you believe you're being charged incorrectly after a legitimate cancellation, raise a dispute with your bank (credit card dispute or direct debit reversal) and lodge a complaint with the Commerce Commission or Consumer NZ.
